QB Environment Reprojection

Full transparency on every modelling decision, data source, assumption, and known limitation in the environment reprojection system.

1 · Overview

A quarterback's raw completion percentage conflates skill with context. Playing in a climate-controlled dome, on a sunny autumn afternoon, or in a driving snowstorm all shift the completion-probability surface independently of the QB's talent. This tool removes those environmental contributions so analysts can compare QBs on a common baseline.

The reprojection formula is: 

Comp%_neutral = Comp%_observed − Σ (effect_i × exposure_i)

where each effect_i is a learned regression coefficient (shrunk toward the league mean) and exposure_i is the fraction of a QB's dropbacks under condition i.

3 · Feature Engineering

3.1 · Dome Indicator

Each stadium is classified as dome (always closed), retractable (partially indoor), or outdoor. Retractable-roof stadiums are assigned a historical open/closed fraction derived from the nflverse stadium metadata. A per-play dome flag is computed and aggregated to a per-QB seasonal dome snap share.

3.2 · Weather Buckets

Outdoor plays are bucketed into five weather conditions:

  • Clear / Mild — baseline (wind <15 mph, temp ≥35 °F, no precipitation)
  • High Wind — wind speed ≥15 mph
  • Rain — precipitation flag set, temp ≥35 °F
  • Cold — temp <35 °F, no precipitation
  • Snow — precipitation flag set, temp <35 °F

Dome plays are always assigned Clear / Mild regardless of reported weather, preventing dome roof status from double-counting.

3.3 · Covariates in Regression

To isolate environment from talent and usage, the regression conditions on:

  • Intended air yards (binned into 5 buckets)
  • Down & distance bucket (short/medium/long)
  • Coverage shell (man / zone / unknown)
  • Season (fixed effects)

4 · Shrinkage Model

Raw environment coefficients are estimated via an OLS regression on individual pass attempts (binary outcome: complete / incomplete). Coefficient estimates are then shrunk toward the cross-sample mean using an empirical Bayes approach: 

θ̂_shrunk = (n_i / (n_i + κ)) × θ̂_raw + (κ / (n_i + κ)) × μ̄

where n_i is the sample size for condition i, κ is the prior pseudo-count (tuned by cross-validation), and μ̄ is the grand-mean effect across conditions. This prevents the model from over-fitting to small-sample weather combinations (e.g., the handful of snow games each season).

Key assumption: Environment effects are additive and separable from QB skill. The model does not attempt to capture QB × Environment interactions (e.g., a QB who is uniquely bad in wind). Such interactions are typically within the noise at current sample sizes.

6 · Validation

Model quality is assessed via:

  1. Leave-one-season-out CV — train on seasons T–1 and back, evaluate on season T.
  2. Year-over-year stability — Pearson correlation of environment-neutral Comp% across adjacent seasons vs. raw Comp%.
  3. Placebo test — null model (no environment adjustment) used as baseline.

7 · Known Limitations

  • Weather data quality. nflverse weather fields are sourced from game-level stadium reports, not play-level sensor data. In-game changes (e.g., wind shifting at halftime) are not captured.
  • Receiver quality. The model does not adjust for receiver separation or drop rate, which correlate with team-level environment (roster construction) rather than QB environment per se.
  • Interaction effects. Additive separability is assumed. A QB who significantly outperforms his peers in bad weather would have his skill attributed to environment, not the reverse. Interaction terms require substantially more data to estimate reliably.
  • Playoff exclusion. Postseason games are excluded from coefficient estimation to avoid survivorship bias (only certain teams play in cold-weather postseason games).
