NFL environment reprojection

Audit of the build decisions

Every consequential choice, the reason for it, the option it beat, and where it is weak. Written in a hostile posture: assume the soft spot until shown otherwise.

← Back to the tool

Summary

This audits the build, not the code style. The tool is a stylized projection, not a causal or predictive model, and it says so on its face. Its foundation holds: a dome lifts completion percentage by about two and a half to three points, re-derived from the play-by-play and corroborated by independent public sources. The single real error during the build was a shrinkage step that flattened every quarterback to the league mean. It was caught and fixed. The home-field confound flagged in the first version is now removed: the environment effect is measured on road games only, so home-field advantage no longer rides along inside it. The correction is not cosmetic. Drew Brees, long seen as a dome quarterback, drops from a +3.8 raw split to a +2.4 clean effect once his home dome games are set aside. Most of his dome edge was home-field advantage. One weakness remains: the environment axis is really weather, not the roof.

Decisions and their soft spots

Counterfactual method: shrinkage blend

Each quarterback's own dome-minus-outdoor split is blended toward the league average, weighted by sample size. It beat a pure league transform, which is too flat, and a player-anchored split, which is too noisy on thin samples.

Soft spot

It assumes every quarterback is drawn from one league-wide distribution of effects. They are not. Offenses and styles differ, and the shrinkage softens that assumption without removing it.

The environment effect is measured on road games only

A quarterback's home games are all one environment, so pooling home and away lets home-field advantage ride along inside the environment number. The effect is therefore measured on away games only, where both environments are played away and home field cancels. The anchor, his real career completion percentage and real environment mix, still uses all of his games, so the displayed number is his true career rate.

Resolved

This was the sharp soft spot in the first version, which pooled home and away. Fixing it matched the Sports Info Solutions method and moved real numbers. Drew Brees drops from a +3.8 raw split to a +2.4 clean effect, so most of his dome edge was home field. The cost is a shorter roster, 79 quarterbacks instead of 106, and wider bands, which fit the tool's thesis that the swings are small and uncertain.

The axis is labeled environment, but the driver is weather

Dome versus outdoor is used as the control because it is the documented, public split.

Soft spot

Roof bundles temperature, wind, and rain together with the dome. Public analysis shows cold below forty degrees and rain are what move completion percentage, not the roof itself. The effect is not caused by the roof alone, and it is not causal.

Games-started filter: 8 qualifying road games per environment

A qualifying game is 10 or more attempts. The minimum is two-sided and applied to road games, so no quarterback makes the roster on a lopsided or home-heavy sample.

Soft spot

Attempts are a proxy for starts. A blowout finished by a backup could count. Low impact.

Metric and window: completion percentage only, 1999–2024

One metric, regular season, twenty-six seasons.

Soft spot

It ignores yards, sacks, interceptions, and the deep ball, where the dome edge is largest. Era pooling assumes the delta is stable across all those seasons. The delta is more stable than the raw level, but not guaranteed constant.

The error that was caught

The first shrinkage estimator returned a between-quarterback variance of zero. Every quarterback collapsed to the league mean, so every counterfactual came out identical and the pick-a-player mechanic was fake. The cause was a weighted method-of-moments step that let large samples drive the estimated spread to zero. Switching to DerSimonian-Laird, the standard meta-analysis estimator, recovered a real spread of 1.6 points in the original home-and-away model and significant heterogeneity; the current road-only model puts the spread at 1.7. The lesson is the useful part: a flat result that makes the product pointless is a signal to check the estimator, not a reason to ship the flatness.

What was not audited

  • No causal identification. This is a descriptive split presented as a counterfactual, and it is framed that way.
  • Weather severity, altitude, turf, and crowd noise are not separated from the roof.
  • The nflverse roof and stadium flags are trusted as given, not re-checked game by game.
  • Playoffs are excluded. Regular season only. Quarterbacks only. Completion percentage only.

Verdict

Ready to ship as a stylized projection tool, with the framing kept on its face. The home-field confound is now removed by measuring the effect on road games only. One caveat remains and is worth stating plainly: the driver is weather, not the roof, so dome versus outdoor bundles temperature, wind, and rain together with the enclosure. That is fine for a portfolio tool. It would need separating if this were ever raised to a research claim.
