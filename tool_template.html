How this is built

The counterfactual shifts the quarterback's real completion percentage by his environment effect. That effect is not his raw dome-minus-outdoor split. It is his own split, measured on road games only so home-field advantage does not contaminate it, blended toward the league average and weighted by sample size. The blend is empirical Bayes shrinkage; the spread of true effects across quarterbacks, which sets the weights, is estimated with the DerSimonian-Laird method from random-effects meta-analysis, treating each quarterback's split as a small study. A quarterback with many road games in both settings keeps most of his own effect. A thin road sample is pulled toward the league. The anchor, his real career completion percentage and his real environment mix, uses all of his games, so the number you see is his true career rate.

The band widens as you drag away from his real environment mix, because the projection gets less certain the further it sits from what he actually did. At his real mix the line equals his real career number. That is the point the whole tool is anchored to.
